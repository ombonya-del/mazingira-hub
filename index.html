Kenya's own exposure

80% of Kenya's 2035 climate pledge is conditional on international finance that COP30 did not secure.

Kenya's second NDC commits to cutting emissions 35% below business-as-usual by 2035 — 75.25 MtCO₂e. Only 20% is funded domestically. The pledge carries a USD 56 billion price tag. Belém set a target to triple adaptation finance by 2035, with no interim milestone and no burden-sharing formula. The money and the deadline do not line up.

      Circulating on Xspreading after the Magadi shutdown · this week

      "Kenya just shut down Africa's biggest soda ash producer and put 1,000+ jobs at risk over 'compliance' paperwork. This is exactly the anti-investment bureaucracy that keeps Africa poor."

      🔗The fuller pictureCitizen Digital · Tata Chemicals Magadi suspension ↗
      ↻ amplifying in reply threads · this week🔗 View original in raia →
      False dilemma
      ↳ Coalition rebuttal

      "Jobs or compliance" is a false choice. The suspension order lists unpaid mineral royalties, failure to honour Community Development Agreements, weak local hiring and skills transfer, and environmental-compliance shortcomings at Lake Magadi — a fragile Rift Valley soda lake. Requiring a company that has mined a public resource since 1911 to pay what it owes and meet its community and environmental obligations is the benefit-sharing the Constitution's Article 69 demands, not "anti-jobs bureaucracy." The real risk to those 1,000 jobs is a business model that treats compliance as optional.

      𝕏
      Jusper Machogu@JusperMachogu · 14 Jul 2026

      "A friend just shared this amazing article. Once people visit Africa, they begin to witness and experience energy poverty. Solving energy poverty solves all sorts of poverty — hunger, housing, water, manufacturing, transportation, etc."

      ↻ 30 · ♥ 63 · 👁 1.8kon raia ↗
      Bait-and-switch
      ↳ Coalition rebuttal

      Energy poverty is real — but this is the "Fossil Fuels for Africa" account, and "solving energy poverty" is its standard setup for arguing the fix is coal, oil and gas. It isn't: Africa's fastest, cheapest gains are coming from the renewables, mini-grids and clean cooking already scaling across the continent, and Kenya's own grid already runs about 90% renewable. Wrapping the dirtiest, slowest option in the language of poverty relief is the bait-and-switch — the same account elsewhere states it plainly: "Fossil Fuels for Africa is how we free their time."

      "You can't halt development for a few trees. Sacrificing 8 acres of Imenti Forest for a lodge, airstrip and roads will actually save the whole Mt Kenya ecosystem — the 'conservationists' fighting it just want to keep Meru poor."

      🔗The fuller pictureThe Standard · Imenti airstrip · public participation ↗
      ↻ amplifying in reply threads · this week🔗 View original in raia →
      False dilemma
      ↳ Coalition rebuttal

      "Development or conservation" is a false choice. Nobody is asking to freeze roads or hospitals — only that projects inside a gazetted public forest follow the law: the public participation and environmental assessment required by Articles 69 and 70 of the Constitution. Those safeguards were skipped, which is why the Green Belt Movement has gone to court and why the government was forced to convene public participation on the airstrip. Clearing forest first and consulting later isn't development; it's skipping the rules that make development lawful.
