## A. Proposed changes to existing entries

### 1. ExxonMobil — Oil major
**Current rating: Lip-service.** Proposed rating: **Lip-service (no change) — refresh the "reality" text.**
Rationale: fresh litigation conduct reinforces, rather than changes, the existing rating — Exxon is now using the courts to resist emissions disclosure and shareholder pressure.

- **Promised (unchanged):** An "ambition" for net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions from its operated (Scope 1&2) assets by 2050.
- **Proposed reality update:** Still the only Western supermajor with no Scope 3 (product-emissions) target. In late October 2025 it sued California to strike down climate-disclosure laws SB 253 and SB 261 ahead of the 2026 reporting deadlines, and in January 2026 filed a Texas court complaint seeking to block an investor Scope-3 resolution from even reaching a shareholder vote.
- **Why change the text:** the current "reality" cites the Pioneer acquisition and the Scope-1&2-only framing; the proposed text adds the newer, well-sourced pattern of litigating against disclosure and shareholder accountability.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.esgtoday.com/exxon-sues-california-to-stop-new-climate-reporting-laws/
  - https://www.esgdive.com/news/exxonmobil-sues-california-over-climate-laws-alleging-free-speech-violations-sb-253-261/804425/

### 2. World Bank — Public finance
**Current rating: Partial.** Proposed: **Partial (no change yet) — add a WATCH note to the "reality" text.**
Rationale: a confirmed return to upstream-gas financing would weaken the "a real line held" claim and push toward Lip-service, but as of the sources found the gas reversal is still under board debate, not decided.

- **Promised (unchanged):** To stop financing upstream oil & gas after 2019; steer 45% of lending to climate.
- **Proposed reality addition:** In June 2025 the Bank's board lifted its long-standing ban on financing nuclear energy and opened a formal review that is considering reversing its 2017 phase-out of support for upstream gas production. The nuclear move is a genuine clean-firm-power shift; the gas review is the risk to watch.
- **Explicit framing:** Currently Partial → keep Partial, but flag that if the Bank formally re-enters upstream gas financing, the coalition should revisit toward Lip-service.
- **Sources:**
  - https://energynews.oedigital.com/energy-markets/2025/06/11/world-bank-will-end-nuclear-energy-ban-but-still-debate-upstream-gas
  - https://www.brettonwoodsproject.org/2025/07/world-bank-energy-strategy-review-considers-reversing-phaseout-of-support-for-gas-production/

### 3. EACOP entries — reality refresh (two entries affected)
Applies to **"Global insurers & banks (EACOP)"** (current: Kept) and **"Afreximbank & the EACOP African lenders"** (current: Lip-service). **No rating change proposed for either.**
Rationale: the Western finance wall held (Kept stays), and the African-lender syndicate proceeding stays Lip-service — but the shared "reality" framing that the wall "stalled the pipeline's funding" now needs nuance.

- **Key fact:** The pipeline reached roughly 79–82% completion in the first half of 2026 and sponsors target first oil in the second half of 2026, after the African/Middle-Eastern lender syndicate (Afreximbank, Standard Bank, Stanbic Uganda, KCB Bank Uganda, ICD) closed the first external financing tranche.
- **Proposed edit:** soften "stalled the pipeline's funding" to reflect that the refusals delayed and raised the cost of financing but did not stop the project, which is now nearing completion.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/how-eacop-sponsors-finally-got-usd-5bn-financing-sewn-up-5255994
  - https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/0704-54477-uganda-s-eacop-pipeline-reaches-82-completion-moves-closer-to-start-up

## B. New candidates for the index

### Candidate 1 — KCB Group
**Type: Bank · Kenya.** Proposed rating (coalition's read): **Partial.**
Rationale: genuine, sizeable green-finance delivery at group level, undercut by its Ugandan subsidiary sitting inside the EACOP financing syndicate.

- **Promised:** To direct 25% of total lending to green/sustainable projects and expand climate finance across East Africa.
- **Reality:** Disbursed KSh48.8bn in green loans in 2025, reaching 25.84% of lending (up from 21.6% in 2024) and surpassing its own target, and secured a $96.9m (KSh12.5bn) Green Climate Fund facility — yet KCB Bank Uganda is a named lender in the EACOP first-financing-tranche syndicate.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2026/06/kcb-green-loans-rise-to-sh48-8bn/
  - https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001550093/kcb-disburses-sh49b-green-loans-screens-sh588b-for-regional-financing
  - (EACOP syndicate membership) https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/how-eacop-sponsors-finally-got-usd-5bn-financing-sewn-up-5255994

### Candidate 2 — Equity Group (Equity Bank Kenya)
**Type: Bank · Kenya.** Proposed rating (coalition's read): **Partial** (lean positive; recommend confirming no material fossil exposure before finalising).
Rationale: strong, verifiable clean-energy and climate-adaptation financing record; rating held at Partial only pending a check that there is no offsetting fossil financing.

- **Promised:** To mobilise climate finance and accelerate the clean-energy transition, with KSh20bn+ earmarked for climate financing.
- **Reality:** Disbursed roughly KSh24.7bn (~$185m) in climate-related loans and grants in 2023 across renewable energy, energy efficiency and adaptation; co-leads the Africa Rural Climate Adaptation Finance Mechanism (ARCAFIM) with IFAD; recognised by the IFC for climate-finance reporting.
- **Sources:**
  - https://nation.africa/kenya/brand-book/equity-climate-change-mitigation-through-green-financing-3371868
  - https://equitygroupholdings.com/ke/newsroom/press-releases/equity-bank-kenya-recognized-for-climate-financing-reporting-excellence-by-international-finance-corporation/
