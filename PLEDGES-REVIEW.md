## A. Proposed updates to existing entries

### 1. Standard Chartered — Bank — **Currently "Kept" → coalition may consider "Partial"**
- **Rationale:** The "Kept" rating rests narrowly on StanChart's confirmed refusal to finance EACOP, which remains true. But the 2026 *Banking on Climate Chaos* report finds the bank **increased** fossil-fuel financing ~28% in 2025 (against European peers cutting), and its coal-exclusion policy still permits financing of Adani and Indonesia's PLN. Against its own net-zero-by-2050 pledge, the holistic record is weaker than a single "Kept" implies.
- **Promised:** Net-zero financed emissions by 2050; case-by-case fossil due diligence (it declined EACOP).
- **Reality:** The EACOP refusal still holds, but StanChart's overall fossil financing rose ~28% in 2025 and it continues to fund coal-expansion clients such as Adani and PLN through policy loopholes.
- **Proposed change:** Keep the EACOP fact, but the coalition should decide whether the entry stays "Kept" (EACOP-specific) or moves to "Partial" (whole-book behaviour). My read: **Partial** is more defensible now.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.ran.org/press-releases/bocc26/
  - https://www.banktrack.org/article/agm_report_standard_chartered_called_out_for_bankrolling_dangerous_fossil_fuel_projects

### 2. Afreximbank & the EACOP African lenders — Banks · pan-African — **Stays "Lip-service" (reality-text update)**
- **What's new:** **China EximBank is now confirmed** as an EACOP financier (confirmed by Uganda's energy minister and reported via the Business & Human Rights Resource Centre), joining Afreximbank, Stanbic, KCB Uganda, Standard Bank and the ICD. The pipeline passed ~79% completion at the start of 2026 with first oil targeted for H2 2026 — African and Chinese lenders are closing the gap Western finance left.
- **Promised:** Pan-African financiers marketing themselves as responsible engines of African development, several with stated climate/sustainability commitments.
- **Reality:** After 40+ global banks and 30+ insurers ruled EACOP out, the African-led syndicate — now joined by China EximBank — kept the pipeline financed and on track for first oil in H2 2026.
- **Proposed change:** Add China EximBank and the ~79%-complete / H2-2026-first-oil status to the reality text. No rating change.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/uganda-china-eximbank-islamic-development-bank-and-afrexim-bank-confirmed-as-financiers-of-controversial-east-african-crude-oil-pipeline/
  - https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/how-eacop-sponsors-finally-got-usd-5bn-financing-sewn-up-5255994

### 3. World Bank — Public finance — **Stays "Partial" (reality-text update)**
- **What's new:** Beyond dropping the 45% climate-finance target (already noted in the entry), the Bank has **ended its long-standing ban on funding nuclear power** and signed a partnership with the IAEA to support nuclear — including small modular reactors — in developing countries. This is directly relevant to Kenya, which has stated nuclear ambitions. It cuts both ways: potential low-carbon baseload, but a clear move away from the renewables-first framing.
- **Promised:** Exit upstream oil & gas after 2019; steer lending toward climate.
- **Reality:** It held the upstream oil & gas exit, but dropped its sharpest climate-finance metric (45%) in 2025–26 and, in 2026, reopened nuclear financing via an IAEA partnership aimed at African energy access.
- **Proposed change:** Add the nuclear-ban reversal / IAEA partnership to the reality text. No rating change.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/world-bank-and-iaea-backing-new-nuclear-for-development
  - https://www.cgdev.org/blog/world-bank-goes-nuclear

### 4. African Development Bank — Development bank — **Stays "Partial" (reality-text update)**
- **What's new:** Mauritania's **Sidi Ould Tah was elected the Bank's new president (2025)**. Under the new leadership the AfDB has reaffirmed it will keep financing gas as a "transition fuel"; in March 2026 UN human-rights experts urged it to halt all fossil-fuel financing as inconsistent with its own Climate Change & Green Growth Strategy 2021–2030. It continues genuine green lending (e.g. a $150m green-finance package with KCB Kenya).
- **Promised:** New Deal on Energy for Africa — universal access by 2030, renewables-led, no new coal.
- **Reality:** No new coal and a renewables-heavy book, but the new president's AfDB is holding to gas financing as "transition," drawing a March 2026 rebuke from UN human-rights experts.
- **Proposed change:** Refresh reality text with the leadership change and the 2026 gas/UN-criticism developments. No rating change.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.climatechangenews.com/2025/05/30/civil-society-groups-push-back-on-fossil-gas-funding-as-african-development-bank/
  - https://re-course.org/newsupdates/african-development-bank-annual-meetings-2026/

### 5. ExxonMobil — Oil major — **Stays "Lip-service" (reality-text update)**
- **What's new:** Exxon **sued the state of California (Oct 2025)** to strike down its climate-disclosure laws (SB 253 / SB 261) before 2026 reporting deadlines, and its earlier lawsuit against shareholder proponents forced the **withdrawal of a Scope 3 emissions resolution**. In 2026 it also moved to challenge EU carbon-capture rules through investor-state arbitration.
- **Promised:** "Ambition" for net-zero Scope 1&2 from operated assets by 2050.
- **Reality:** Still covers only ~15% of emissions (no Scope 3), and is now using litigation — suing California over emissions-disclosure laws and pressuring shareholder proponents to drop a Scope 3 vote — to resist broader accountability.
- **Proposed change:** Add the litigation record to the reality text. No rating change.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/natural-gas/102725-exxonmobil-sues-california-over-climate-disclosure-laws
  - https://insideclimatenews.org/news/28072026/exxonmobil-isds-challenge-eu-carbon-capture/

### 6. TotalEnergies — Oil major — **Stays "Partial" (reality-text refresh, newer figures)**
- **What's new:** The **2026 Sustainability & Climate progress report** reports a 65% methane reduction and an 18.6% cut in lifecycle carbon intensity vs 2015 (exceeding the 2025 target), while restating that it "cannot formulate net-zero targets" under EU rules because 1.5°C is "out of reach." It continues to expand oil & gas and holds a 62% stake in EACOP.
- **Promised:** Carbon neutrality by 2050; falling carbon-intensity of energy sold.
- **Reality:** Real operational progress (methane –65%, lifecycle intensity –18.6% vs 2015) but no net-zero commitment under EU rules and continued expansion, including its 62% lead stake in EACOP.
- **Proposed change:** Update the figures to the 2026 report; no rating change.
- **Sources:**
  - https://onestopesg.com/esg-news/totalenergies-posts-65-methane-reduction-and-18-6-lifecycle-carbon-intensity-drop-exceeding-2025-targets
  - https://www.urgewald.org/en/totalenergies-pr-2026

## B. New candidates (not yet in the list)

### Candidate 1 — Africa Energy Bank (AEB) — Bank · pan-African — proposed **"Lip-service"** *(see caveat)*
- **Rationale:** A new pan-African institution built expressly to fund oil & gas where Western capital has pulled back. Directly reshapes who finances African fossil projects, which is core to this index.
- **Promised:** Championed by APPO and Afreximbank, the AEB frames itself around African-led, "orderly" energy development and energy access — but its climate/sustainability commitment is thin and largely rhetorical.
- **Reality:** Launched 1 July 2026 in Abuja with ~$5bn capital (targeting $10bn phase one, ~$15bn by 2030), it is explicitly positioned as an alternative to Western lenders that restrict fossil finance, prioritising upstream, refining, gas-to-power and cross-border pipelines in Angola, Nigeria and Libya.
- **Caveat for reviewers:** The index rates entities against *their own* climate promises. The AEB's climate commitment is weak enough that it may fit better as a "watch/context" note than a scored entry. Flagging for the coalition to decide.
- **Sources:**
  - https://energycapitalpower.com/africa-energy-bank-targets-15b-by-2030-signaling-new-era-of-continental-investment/
  - https://africa-news-agency.com/energy-africa-energy-bank-to-launch-on-july-1-2026-with-5-billion-capital/

### Candidate 2 — KCB Group — Bank · Kenya — proposed **"Partial"**
- **Rationale:** Kenya's largest bank, with a formal net-zero pledge and real green lending, yet its Ugandan subsidiary is a named EACOP lender — a clear promise-vs-reality tension, highly relevant to Kenya/East Africa.
- **Promised:** Group-wide Net Zero by 2050 and a target to grow green/sustainable lending to 25% of the portfolio by 2031.
- **Reality:** It is scaling genuine green finance (e.g. a $150m AfDB green-lending package), but KCB Bank Uganda sits in the EACOP syndicate that closed the pipeline's first $755m financing tranche — funding fossil infrastructure while pledging net zero.
- **Sources:**
  - https://kcbgroup.com/sustainability-page
  - https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business-tech/how-eacop-sponsors-finally-got-usd-5bn-financing-sewn-up-5255994

### Candidate 3 — China EximBank — Public finance · China — proposed **"Lip-service"**
- **Rationale:** A state policy bank central to closing EACOP's financing gap after Western lenders exited, set against China's stated "green Belt and Road" / no-new-coal-abroad posture.
- **Promised:** Operates under China's stated commitments to green overseas investment and (at state level) to build no new coal power abroad.
- **Reality:** Confirmed by Uganda's government as a financier of EACOP, helping mobilise ~$3bn of Chinese debt for the crude-oil pipeline — extending fossil-infrastructure finance in East Africa even as China markets a greener overseas footprint.
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/uganda-china-eximbank-islamic-development-bank-and-afrexim-bank-confirmed-as-financiers-of-controversial-east-african-crude-oil-pipeline/
